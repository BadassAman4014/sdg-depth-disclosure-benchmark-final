3.4.2  Breadth of SDG engagement
The study preferred using manual coding similar to other comparable studies such as 
[16, 43] as some of the reports were in image format which are resistant to software anal­
ysis. The study used sentence as well as image as recording units as it is customary for 
companies to use iconography to report SDGs to enhance the visual appeal of the cor­
porate reports. The reports were analyzed with respect to the presence of all or some of 
the given words or corresponding icons: Sustainable development goal(s), SDG(s), SDG 
followed by the number with or without space (e.g. SDG 13 or SDG13), title of each of 
the SDG (e.g. “Zero Hunger”) or specified icon designated for each of the SDG. A similar 
scheme of words has been used by Galeazzo et al. [23] for generating codes to estimate 
breadth of SDG engagement. A complete coding sheet is given in appendix Table 4.
Following this a priori coding sheet, two authors separately performed the coding pro­
cess simultaneously and periodically came together to check intercoder reliability and to 
remove discrepancies until a consensus-based scoring was achieved across the sample.
Since 12 companies in the sample have used the term SDG without mentioning any 
specific SDG, we have created a separate category for “SDG in general” in addition to 
17 categories for specific SDGs. We analyzed the corporate reports to identify these cat­
egories, coding them as 1 if a relevant term or icon appears in the report (regardless of 
its frequency) and 0 otherwise. Then, the codes are used to find breadth of SDG engage­
ment i.e. the number of sampled firms that are committed to each SDG. Scores closer to 
98 indicate a high level of SDG engagement, while scores closer to 0 indicate a low level.

In addition to the global prioritization of some of the SDGs previously discussed, the 
PSX’s focus on six goals, namely SDG5, SDG6, SDG7, SDG8, SDG9 and SDG12, and its 
emphasis on minimum reporting requirements for listed companies also provide a plau­
sible explanation for the findings. Except for SDG 9, which has an intermediate breadth 
of engagement, other five SDGs have the highest breadth of engagement.
4.3  Depth of SDG engagement
KSE-100 companies exhibit low depth of SDG engagement (Fig. 5). 62 companies have 
no targets, actions, or measurement of outcome related to the SDGs, indicating zero 
depth of engagement. In some instances, the companies mention SDG in their reports 
without taking further steps. The symbolic engagement is substantiated by recent related 
work including Perryman et al. [49] and Heras-Saizarbitoria et al. [26].
The SDG-wise depth scores remained low in all cases. Specifically, SDGs with score 3 
or above include SDG7(3.60) followed by SDG10 (3.25), SDG13 (3.09), SDG11 (3.07) and 
SDG4, SDG3, and SDG2. The SDGs with score less than 3 include SDG14 (1.83), SDG1 
(2.00), SDG15 (2.00), SDG17 (2.00), SDG16 (2.30), SDG9 (2.58), SDG5 (2.90), SDG12, 
SDG6, and SDG8 with scores of 2.95 each. It is noteworthy that these results align with 
recent similar studies such as [28, 43, 49].
