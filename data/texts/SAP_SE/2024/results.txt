Executive Summary 
The Standard Application Process (SAP) is a flagship effort to streamline and transform how data users 
apply for access to Federal confidential data. To ensure ongoing leadership and support for the SAP, the 
Office of Management and Budget’s (OMB) Memorandum Establishment of Standard Application 
Process Requirements on Recognized Statistical Agencies and Units (M-23-04)) established the SAP 
Governance Board as the executive steering committee for the SAP. This 2024 SAP Annual Report is 
required under the SAP Governance Board (SAP-GB) Charter which specifies that, in fulfillment of its 
responsibility to monitor the operation of the SAP, the SAP-GB will deliver to the Director of OMB and to 
the Interagency Council of Statistical Policy (ICSP) an annual report of SAP activities that addresses 
performance, provides recommendations, and identifies opportunities to improve the efficiency of the 
SAP.  
Key accomplishments in Calendar Year 2024 include: 
• 
The continued operation of a standardized process for individuals to apply to access available 
conﬁdential data assets for evidence-building purposes. In 2024, 499 conﬁdential data access 
applications were submitted through the SAP portal. The total number of applications submitted 
since the SAP portal’s launch in December 2022 is 944.  
• 
Compliance with the review time limits outlined in the SAP policy. The average time for agencies 
to review a single-agency application was 65 days (within the 84 days permitted per M-23-04); 
the average time to review a multi-agency application was 99 days (within the 168 days 
permitted). Average review times are calculated over the period of time from launch (December 
2022) through 2024. 
• 
Updates to the SAP Data Catalog, which lists 1540 conﬁdential data assets for which users may 
request access.  
• 
Finalization of a Communications Plan to inform an overall strategy for stakeholder engagement 
and messaging about the SAP. 
• 
Release of four new features on the SAP portal to comply with policy requirements and enhance 
the user experience: detailed metrics reported to the public; application appeals; application 
auto-withdrawals; and co-principal investigator edit ability.  
• 
Delivery of four quarterly public newsletters, with over 4,000 newsletter subscribers by the end 
of 2024, one public webinar, and 2 listening sessions.  
• 
Publication of six new SAP learning resources.  
• 
Reached consensus on an expanded set of SAP metrics to be reported to the public.  
Goals for 2025 and beyond include:  
• 
Continue SAP portal site enhancements and improvements to the SAP user experience.  
• 
Further integrate with related data ecosystem eﬀorts, such as the National Secure Data Service 
(NSDS) Demonstration Project and the Federal Statistical Research Data Center (FSRDC) network.  
• 
Expand agency participation in the SAP by ﬁnalizing the development and approval of an SAP 
membership application package and enabling additional agencies to request to join the SAP as 
data owners. 
• 
Ensure all SAP-participating agencies are adhering to SAP legislative and policy requirements and 
provide support to remedy any areas of non-compliance.

Support SAP resource needs by developing SAP budget recommendations for agency

Background  
The Foundations for Evidence-Based Policymaking Act of 2018 (also referred to as the Evidence Act) 
required a Standard Application Process (SAP) be established for requests for confidential data from 
Federal agencies covered under the Confidential Protection and Statistical Efficiency Act (CIPSEA). 
Section 3583 of the Evidence Act calls for a common application, standard review criteria, timeframes 
for prompt determinations, reporting metrics for transparency, an appeals process for negative 
determinations, and outreach to stakeholder communities. Following the passage of the legislation, 
OMB published a policy memorandum entitled Establishment of Standard Application Process 
Requirements on Recognized Statistical Agencies or Units (M-23-04) in December 2022, which outlines 
process requirements for agencies implementing the SAP.1 Concurrent with the policy, the SAP portal 
was launched, and the SAP Governance Board, under the purview of the ICSP, was convened. 
Working within the requirements and guiding principles of both the legislation and policy, the SAP-GB, 
including its working groups, seek to further enhance the SAP portal, meet the needs of stakeholders, 
and ensure all requirements of both the legislation and policy continue to be met as the SAP evolves. 
This annual report provides an overview of SAP activities and accomplishments for the Calendar Year 
2024. 
The SAP is an interagency effort led by OMB and the ICSP to implement a shared service across the 
Federal statistical system, per the Evidence Act. The U.S. Chief Statistician provides key leadership for 
the SAP, as an OMB senior executive who serves as the chair of the ICSP and co-chair of the SAP-GB. The 
SAP-GB, as required by M-23-04, provides executive oversight and guidance of the SAP, while its 
interagency working groups are tasked with executing technical, communications, budget, and policy 
requirements of the SAP. A project management office (PMO), the National Center for Science and 
Engineering Statistics, provides project integration and support across the effort.  
The SAP portal was launched and began accepting applications on December 8, 2022. The SAP portal is 
comprised of the SAP Data Catalog (a searchable metadata inventory of confidential Federal data

implementation in support of the U.S. Federal statistical system's critical longstanding — and expanding 
— role for supporting evidence-based decision-making.

Per M-23-04, agencies adjudicate applications using the following standard review criteria: 
Figure 6: Aggregate approval rate (Dec 2022 – Dec 2024) 
• 
Statistical purpose: Project proposals must have a statistical purpose 
• 
Allowed use: The data use described in the project proposal must be consistent with any 
restrictions on use of a speciﬁc data asset. 
• 
Statistical disclosure limitation: Project proposals must be able to apply sound statistical 
disclosure limitation approaches and techniques. 
• 
Demonstrated need: Project proposals must demonstrate a need for conﬁdential data and show 
that public data assets are insuﬃcient to accomplish the project’s goals. 
• 
Feasibility: A project’s goals must be achievable with the conﬁdential data assets requested.  
• 
Maintaining public trust: Project proposals must support an agency’s ability to maintain trust 
and credibility among data users and providers. 
SAP Activities and Accomplishments for 2024 
The SAP portal completed its second full year of operation in 2024. The governance framework of the 
SAP is a cornerstone of its success, leveraging expertise from across the Federal statistical system 
through interagency working groups and backed by a robust Project Management Office. See Appendix 
1 for a list of current SAP Governance Board and SAP working group member agencies.  
SAP Governance Board 
The SAP-GB serves as the executive steering committee for the SAP and operates as a subcommittee of 
the full ICSP. It serves dual roles: to represent the Federal agencies and units utilizing the SAP and to 
assist OMB in the oversight and coordination of the SAP. This includes identifying opportunities to

SAP Governance Board Accomplishments:

Approved the establishment of a new Budget and Policy sub-working group to streamline data

by a contractor, the PMO is responsible for selecting the contractor and monitoring contractor 
performance. As noted earlier in this report, ICPSR is the current technical contractor for the SAP portal.

Conclusion 
The SAP has reshaped the process to apply for access to Federal confidential data. It has increased 
transparency and standardization by providing a comprehensive listing of Federal data assets through 
the SAP Data Catalog, implementing a standard application form, and applying common review criteria 
to adjudicate applications. The SAP is built on the partnership and shared expertise of the Federal 
Statistical System and the individual hard work of agency employees. Collaboration and consensus 
across agencies are key to the ongoing success of the SAP.  
As the SAP enters its third year of operation, the SAP-GB seeks to prioritize the continued development 
of the SAP. Critical areas to support include expanding SAP membership to new data-providing agencies, 
improving the SAP portal user experience, and streamlining data security training. 
The SAP-GB also looks to strengthen the SAP user community through engagement events and ensuring 
that users have a means to share feedback, learn from each other, and share research insights. The SAP-
GB actively monitors the evolving data landscape to identify interdependencies with related efforts, 
such as the FSRDC network, the NSDS Demonstration Project, and the implementation of other Evidence 
Act requirements as well as additional Federal policies and regulations. The SAP-GB looks forward to and 
relies on the continued support of OMB and the ICSP as the SAP evolves and improves.

Appendix 3 – List of SAP Portal Features Developed in 2024 
Released to the Public on SAP Portal: 
1. Public Reporting (Detailed): 
a. Description: Gives the public access to detailed application metrics and summary 
information regarding requests for data.  
b. Developed in 2023; tested and released in 2024. 
c. Rationale: SAP policy. 
2. Application Appeals: 
a. Description: Allows applicants to ﬁle an appeal regarding an adverse application 
determination (i.e., non-approved project).  
b. Developed in 2023; tested and released in 2024. 
c. Rationale: SAP policy. 
3. Application Auto-Withdrawals: 
a. Description:  
i. Automatically withdraws applications in “Draft” status if they are inactive for 6 
months. Automatically withdraws applications in “Changes Needed” after 6 
months. 
ii. Developed in 2023; released in 2024. 
b. Rationale: SAP policy. 
4. Co-Principal Investigator Edit Ability: 
a. Description: Allows co-principal investigators (Co-PIs) to edit an SAP application after 
submission.  
b. Released in 2024. 
c. Rationale: Usability enhancement.

Appendix 4 – List of SAP Portal Features to be Developed 
New SAP Portal Features and Functionality (2025): 
To be Developed: 
1. "Provisioned by" Fields: 
a. Description: Data provisioning options (years, geographic unit, etc.) are dynamically 
updated within the SAP application based on the data selected. This allows applicants to 
select from a list of available options rather than entering manually from the SAP Data 
Catalog. It signiﬁcantly reduces user error and revision time and improves usability.  
b. Designed in 2024; to be developed in 2025. 
c. Rationale: Usability enhancement. 
2. Amendments: 
a. Description: Allows researchers to edit approved applications to reﬂect changes to an 
ongoing project. This ensures that the SAP portal and SAP metrics accurately capture 
and report how data are used.  
b. Technical requirements approved in 2024; to be designed and developed in 2025. 
c. Rationale: SAP policy. 
To be Tested and Released: 
3. ORCID: 
a. Description: Adds Open Researcher and Contributor ID (ORCID) to the SAP application.  
b. Developed in 2024; to be released in 2025. 
c. Rationale: Usability enhancement. 
4. Dashboard Search and Sort: 
a. Description: Allows applicants and reviewers to search by keywords and sort applications 
by several metrics on their dashboards.  
b. Developed in 2024; to be released in 2025. 
c. Rationale: Usability enhancement. 
5. Application Download with Comments: 
a. Description: Ability to download an application with reviewer comments.  
b. Developed in 2024; to be released in 2025. 
c. Rationale: Usability enhancement.
