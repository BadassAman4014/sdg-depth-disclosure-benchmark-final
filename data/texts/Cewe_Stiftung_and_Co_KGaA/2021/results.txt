AN INTERVIEW WITH DR CHRISTIAN FRIEGE (CHAIRMAN OF THE BOARD OF  
MANAGEMENT) AND THOMAS MEHLS (DIRECTOR OF SUSTAINABILITY)
2021 was an eventful but also a success-
ful year for CEWE – still, we first need  
to talk once again about the pandemic. 
Let’s be honest: don’t you get flustered?
Dr Christian Friege: I do. No need to  
sugarcoat things. For one, because per-
sonally, I would have wanted nothing 
more than some degree of normal – 
meeting with business partners and col-
leagues, a face-to-face exchange, and 
celebrating success together. I missed  
all of that. 
As for business, the ongoing pandemic 
was not much help to us either. While  
in 2020 we were still able to say that  
we took the opportunity to improve our 
set-up, last year we began to feel a 
decline in orders for the CEWE PHOTO-
BOOK due to people travelling less. But 
we don’t want to complain. As it turned 
out, our partnership-based collaboration 
with our suppliers proved fruitful espe-
cially in those difficult times. Thanks to 
tight coordination, solid interaction and 
the farsighted planning of our CEWE col-
leagues, we were always well stocked 
despite supply shortages. 
But what impressed me the most was  
the flexibility, the enthusiasm and the  
tremendous effort which our workforce 
showed in the face of these adversities. 
That is what made the difference in the 
end, and we are extremely grateful for 
this. 
Thomas Mehls: Very much so. Especially 
during the Christmas season which is cru-
cial for us, when Omicron swept across 
Europe, our colleagues showed great 
prudence and acted very responsibly. The 
health and safety protocols that are in 
place at the production sites did the rest 
so in the end, we made it through the 
month of December with a certain degree 
of ease. Plus, we rolled out a comprehen-
sive vaccination programme at many 
locations of the CEWE Group. After all, 
despite our responsibility to keep the 
production going – the health and safety 
of our employees remains our most 
important obligation.
As the outcome of the latest survey by  
the Great Place to Work research and 
management consultancy shows, this is 
reflected in excellent company values. 
Thomas Mehls: That’s right, identification 
with the company is high. Many of our 
colleagues have been working at CEWE

T
he company’s long-term economic perform­
ance is CEWE’s top priority. Only if we build 
on an economically sound foundation can we 
welcome the challenges that the competition 
and the markets present to us, and look 
towards the future with confidence. CEWE was once again 
distinguished for this in 2021. Both investors and many 
partnering organisations can depend just as much on this 
as our employees. 
CEWE offers a world of quality products. Whether our 
market-leading CEWE PHOTOBOOK, our annual calendars 
or our wall art: almost every CEWE product meets a 
specific customer need and enjoys many years, if not 
decades, of appreciation. All CEWE products are unique 
in their own right. 
In our endeavour to contribute towards SDG 12 (Respon-
sible Consumption and Production), we are continually 
reviewing our entire product portfolio, improving produc-
tion processes, and optimising supply chains. Our pro-
duction is regionally based and we work wherever pos­
sible with local suppliers and treat our partners fairly. 
Moreover, we invest in innovation and education along 
the lines of SDG 4 (Quality Education) in order to shape 
the future of Photofinishing, Retail and Commercial 
Online Printing. 
C
EWE’s core operations are all about paper 
and thus about the renewable resource, 
wood. Therefore, CEWE invests special 
efforts in this field (SDG 13 Climate Action 
and SDG 15 Life on Land) to mitigate its 
ecological footprint, on the one hand, by directly cutting 
resources and CO2 emissions and, on the other, with com-
pensation projects such as reforestation programmes and 
wind farms. 
More specifically, we further expanded our use of 
FSC®-certified products in 2021. All advent calendars are 
now FSC®-certified, as are over 90 % of the digital print-
ing paper and nearly 100 % of the photographic paper. 
CEWE will consistently pursue this path in the years to 
come, so as to obtain the respective quality seal for all 
the paper we use by 2023. 
Moreover, we continue to pursue ambitious goals to 
reduce CO2 emissions. By 2025, CEWE will cut its Scope 1 
and Scope 2 CO2 emissions in half – i. e., those emissions 
that occur from sources owned directly by our compan­
ies, or indirectly by our energy suppliers. In absolute 
terms this corresponds to a reduction from 13,401 t/a to 
less than 6,700 t/a. For emissions that impact the supply 
chain – so-called Scope 3 emissions – we are planning a 
reduction by a minimum of 25 %.

Once every three months, CEWE analyses the risks inher-
ent in its business. On principle, gross risks are identified 
within this context, meaning before the implementation of 
risk mitigation measures. Mitigating risks, such as envi-
ronmental risks or in relation to data protection and data 
security, requires a net observation (once risk mitigation 
measures have been implemented).
A systematic opportunity management and risk approach 
is the ongoing duty of the Board of Management as well 
as leadership task for all responsibilities. Every single 
opportunity and risk are regularly recorded and described.
Their assessment includes the evaluation of their maxi-
mum impact, their probability of occurrence, and the 
resulting expected value. The risk assessment is routinely 
conducted as at December 31. Strategic risks that are 
rated include, for example, the technology and innova-
tion risk as well as the risk from investments in start-ups 
that are close to core operations. Operational risks that 
are reviewed include, for example, general price risks and 
the technical infrastructure.

COMMITTED TO THE ENVIRONMENT
CEWE aims to protect the environment in a very practical 
way. CEWE employees were actively involved in the annual 
“CEWE cleans up” campaign, collecting waste around the 
premises in Oldenburg in the month of November, and for

The locations in Oldenburg and Eschbach work closely 
with the local nature conservation group, NABU. In 2013, 
this cooperation went cross-regional with the Federal 
NABU association. Disclosure 102-13 
The collaboration encompasses shared photography 
competitions, the sponsoring of nature conservation pro-
jects, and the animal-friendly design of the company’s 
premises in Oldenburg. Nesting boxes for songbirds and 
bats have been put up, as well as domes for hedgehogs, 
castles for bumble bees and an insect hotel. Since June 
2019, CEWE has been home to two honeybee colonies. 
Oldenburg’s beekeeper association oversaw the bees in 
their first year and continue to lend their support to the 
project. In the meantime, five employees of CEWE have 
been trained as beekeepers. 
CEWE is financially involved in a special project of the 
nature conservation group, NABU. NABU wishes to acquire 
natural forests that are home to a wealth of different spe-
cies for protected birds in order to create a “Jungle for 
Tomorrow”. Currently, CEWE sponsors the “Hute-
landschaft Altraft-Sonnenburg” project, a 571 hec-
tares-large conservation area near Bad Freienwalde in 
Brandenburg. In 2021, NABU was able to acquire a 38 hec-
tares-large area in the conservation area with the help of 
CEWE. This area is to be upgraded with nature-conserving 
measures. To achieve this, land which to date had been 
used for conventional agriculture will be converted into a 
pesticide-free conservation field which will provide an 
invaluable habitat for insects and skylarks. Moreover, it is 
planned to grow hedges using the domestic cornelian 
cherry or hawthorn in the winter of 2021. They will secure 
a rich offer of nectar and pollen for bees, bumblebees, 
butterflies, hoverflies and many other insects.
