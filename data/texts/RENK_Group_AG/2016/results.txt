What’s it all about? 
RENK is Europe’s leading producer of industrial propulsion systems, dominating the 
fast-growing military segment with a 72% defense revenue share (FY24). With European
equipment defense spending set to rise over 170% by 2030 (mwb. est.) and persistent
equipment shortages, RENK enjoys highly visible, long-term demand. Its EUR 5.9bn
backlog (~5x FY24 sales) and deep ties with OEMs like Rheinmetall and KNDS support
>14% CAGR through 2032. Diversification into marine propulsion and slide bearings 
adds resilience. Core roles on next-gen platforms, rising aftermarket share, and NATO 
procurement acceleration, plus major orders we expect in H1 2026, position RENK for
growth which, however, the market appears to have already more than priced in. RENK
trades at valuation levels of ~50x FY26 P/E and ~25x EV/EBITDA, which in our view 
already discount flawless execution. We initiate coverage of RENK with a price target
of 65.00 EUR and a SELL rating.

High-quality growth, priced for perfection; SELL 
Market leadership & diversification: RENK is Europe’s #1 producer of industrial pro-
pulsion systems, with 72% of revenues tied to defense. It benefits directly from struc-
tural NATO/EU equipment spending, projected to rise >170% by 2030 (mwb est.), and 
from equipment shortfalls across Europe. RENK supplies core systems to OEMs such 
as Rheinmetall and KNDS, both sitting on record-high backlogs. Beyond defense, 
RENK also leads in marine propulsion (naval vessels, ferries) and is a leading supplier 
of slide bearings, ensuring diversified revenue streams. 
 
Strong growth prospects through 2030: Global military budgets are in a structural 
upcycle, particularly in Europe, and RENK stands out as a direct beneficiary. The com-
pany serves as a key supplier for next-generation tank programs (e.g., Leopard 2A8, 
Panther, Lynx), providing strong growth visibility for the coming decades. A EUR 5.9bn 
order backlog (~5x FY24 sales) supports an estimated 14% CAGR through 2032, while 
the aftermarket share is steadily increasing toward ~35% over time (mwb est.). Ex-
pansion into international markets offers additional upside, although execution at one 
major customer remains a swing factor as progress continues to lag. 
 
Ambitious valuation: RENK trades at stretched valuation levels (~50x FY26 P/E and 
~25x EV/EBITDA), which in our view already discount flawless execution. Our fore-
casts, while broadly aligned with management guidance, are slightly more cautious 
than consensus due to the slow ramp-up at a key customer and softer-than-expected 
European defense budget momentum. Relative to peers such as Rheinmetall (20% 
CAGR, ~23% EBIT margin), RENK’s slower topline growth and lower-margin civil expo-
sure make its current premium valuation difficult to justify. 
 
Key investment theme: RENK offers rare visibility within the European defense space, 
underpinned by a substantial backlog, long-term NATO procurement potential, and a 
steadily expanding aftermarket base. However, short-term momentum is at risk as 
large OEMs have hinted at slower order activity. Any related share price weakness 
could provide a more attractive entry into what fundamentally is a promising invest-
ment case. We initiate coverage of RENK with a SELL rating and a target price of EUR 
65.00.

Company background 
Products & services 
Renk Group AG, based in Augsburg, is a manufacturer of advanced drive technolo-
gies, including transmissions, engines, hybrid drive systems, and vehicle suspen-
sions. It supplies specialty transmissions for tanks, frigates, icebreakers, and indus-
trial machinery, alongside suspension systems for military vehicles. The company 
has more than 3.700 employees. Renk operates 19 facilities worldwide with facili-
ties in Germany, Switzerland, the UK, the USA, and India. Originally spun off from 
MAN SE (part of VOW:GR), Renk was acquired by Triton in 2020 and entered the 
public market on February 7, 2024 as a successful turnaround candidate. With an 
installed base of more than 180.000 units RENK also benefits from recurring reve-
nues (~30%). ~61% of European and North American tracked vehicle platforms were 
supplied by RENK. 
Since 2023, Renk has organized its business into three segments: Vehicle Mobility 
Solutions (VMS) for military and heavy-duty applications, Marine & Industry (M&I) 
for marine and industrial drive systems, and Slide Bearings (SB), supporting high-
performance machinery across sectors. With a constant amount of ~30% of after-
market revenues over the past 4 years, RENK has a great amount of predictable rev-
enues. 
 
 
 
 
 
In the FY 2024, RENK achieved a highly positive performance in growth, profitability, 
and liquidity. Order intake increased significantly, with the VMS segment, particu-
larly its military drive solutions, standing out as the key contributor. This resulted in 
consolidated revenues of EUR 1,140m (previous year: EUR 926 m), surpassing ex-
pectations. Adjusted EBIT also rose to EUR 189m (previous year: EUR 150m), sup-
ported by fixed cost degression effects and strong margins from new business. FCF 
more than 4X to EUR 87m, while net debt was reduced to 1.7x due to increased cash 
flows, reflecting a strong financial position. 
 
 
 
VMS: Vehicle Mobility Solutions (60% of revenues) 
 
The VMS segment is a global leader in innovation and technology for transmissions 
in military tracked vehicles. Beyond transmissions, its product portfolio includes en-
gines, suspensions, electric gearboxes, and complete powerpacks tailored for mili-
tary applications. Additionally, VMS is a leading provider of turnkey testing systems 
across various defense and industrial markets. These systems support customer 
R&D, production, and quality assurance processes in sectors such as automotive, 
rail, aerospace, wind energy, and military vehicles. 
 
Operating in over 70 armed forces worldwide (primarily from NATO countries) Renk 
holds a dominant position in its niche as the #1 supplier of vehicle transmissions 
and suspensions supplying parts for more than 80 types of tracked and wheeled 
military systems. Renk’s VMS solutions serve most tank platforms, including the 
German Leopard 2 and the LYNX. Moreover, Renk’s technology is integrated into

M&I: Marine & Industry (29% of revenues) 
 
RENK’s M&I segment offers drive and coupling solutions, establishing itself as a 
technology leader for military naval forces, commercial shipping, and various indus-
trial applications. RENK’s marine portfolio features adaptable drive systems that 
work with a range of propulsions (diesel, gas, hybrid & electric) which is a key ad-
vantage. This adaptability reduces operational risk and increases flexibility for cus-
tomers in diverse applications. RENK’s technology is deployed in frigates and 
superyachts; for instance, the German F125 and Italian FREMM frigates are 
equipped with RENK’s CODELAG cross-connect gear units, which integrates me-
chanical and electric drives.  
 
In a market where many competitors (mainly from China) prioritize high production 
volumes, RENK differentiates itself through its focus on custom, application-spe-
cific solutions, providing tailored development even for smaller production runs. 
Currently, RENK’s marine solutions are in use by naval forces in over 40 countries, 
demonstrating the quality and international reach of its products. 
 
In the industrial sector, RENK offers solutions that support both traditional and 
emerging energy markets. Their products play a crucial role in the hydrogen econ-
omy, where RENK supplies complete transmissions for hydroelectric plants.

SB: Slide Bearings (11% of revenues) 
 
The SB segment provides plain bearing solutions for applications across various 
industries. RENK holds a particularly strong position in the field of standardized 
electric bearings (E-bearings). The product portfolio also includes horizontal, verti-
cal, and tilting-pad bearings, along with comprehensive related services. These bear-
ings are used in ships, power plants, wind turbines, industrial facilities, and other 
critical environments. A key advantage for RENK in this segment is its ability to offer 
not only standardized bearings but also custom-engineered slide bearing solutions 
tailored to specific client needs. 
 
 
 
 
The SB segment of RENK Group demonstrated steady growth in 2024, with order 
intake increasing by 9.8% to EUR 133m and revenue rising by 12.6% to EUR 125m. 
This growth was driven by strong demand for bearings used in electric motors, gen-
erators, and marine applications, as well as positive developments in the aftermar-
ket service for spare parts. Adjusted EBIT for the segment improved significantly, 
growing by 23.5% to EUR 21m. The consistent performance highlights the seg-
ment's ability to meet high product demand while enhancing operational efficiency

Quality 
Customers 
RENK serves a diverse range of customers across the defense and industrial mar-
kets, with a particular focus on those active in military vehicles, naval and civil ma-
rine sectors, cement and plastic production, as well as oil and gas. Additionally, they 
serve the energy generation industry, including the hydrogen sector. Around 70% of 
revenues are generated in the defense segment, underlining the importance of close 
collaboration with key customers. 
Key customers of their military components are OEM´s like KNDS, Thales, Rheinmet-
all, and BAE Systems which supply armed forces like the US Army and Navy, the 
German Army and Navy, the French and UK armies, as well as the South Korean and 
Israeli armies. Israel accounts for around 3% of RENK’s revenues. Without a resolu-
tion to the current export restrictions, these revenues are at risk of being lost over 
the long term. 
The sector is dominated by a limited number of players and RENK being part of most 
large procurement projects. These projects often have long implementation periods 
and involve large production units, leading to plannable revenues. 
• 
VMS: Supplies components to over 70 armed forces worldwide, with a strong 
focus on NATO and EU countries, as well as nations like South Korea, India, and 
Israel. 
• 
M&I: Marine gear units are used by over 40 navies and coast guards globally, 
primarily in NATO countries with an emphasis on the EU. In the industrial seg-
ment, they serve a broad customer base, mainly in Germany and the USA. 
• 
SB: Their bearings are used in large electric drives across various energy gen-
eration applications, resulting in a fragmented global customer base.

Competition 
Vehicle Mobility Solutions Segment: 
The VMS segment is characterized by a select group of competitors, with Allison 
Transmission (ALSN:US) being a prominent player. Allison is the world's largest man-
ufacturer of medium- and heavy-duty fully automatic transmissions. Notably, Alli-
son´s transmissions had been selected by Rheinmetall for the U.S. XM30 tender 
(mainly due to it´s US base). Allison also supplies their parts for the US M1 tank. 
However, RENK still supplies minor parts for the XM30.  
Another competitor is David Brown Santasalo (England), a global provider of me-
chanical power transmission solutions with operations in 27 locations worldwide 
and over 1,100 employees. They also serve high-profile customers such as KNDS 
and BAE Systems, both of whom are also RENK clients. David Brown Santasalo com-
petes with RENK in both the VMS and M&I segments, offering customized solutions.  
SAPA Placencia is a Spanish defense supplier (family owned) based in the Basque 
Country, specializing in transmissions, gearboxes, and propulsion systems for mili-
tary vehicles. The company is the main provider of drivetrains for Spain’s ASCOD 
program and has also supplied components for Leopard 2 upgrades and other Eu-
ropean projects. SAPA has invested in hybrid and electric drive technologies, aiming 
to strengthen its position in next-generation vehicle propulsion. While smaller in 
scale than RENK, it represents a relevant regional competitor in the European mar-
ket, particularly where governments favor domestic sourcing. 
Despite these competitive pressures, RENK has secured strategic wins, including 
being selected for Rheinmetall’s next-generation KF51 Panther, utilizing the RENK 
HSWL 354 transmission and the LYNX IFV (European pendant to the U.S. XM30). 
This indicates that while customers may switch suppliers, RENK remains a preferred 
partner for advanced solutions in key European programs for the majority of OEM´s.  
RENK currently holds about 75% market share in its segment. However, rising com-
petition in the VMS business could pressure margins as rivals pursue aggressive 
pricing. Still, barriers to entry remain very high given substantial capital require-
ments and the strong incumbency of existing players. Geopolitical factors, including 
the preference for domestic or EU/NATO-aligned suppliers, further protect RENK’s 
position and limit competition from lower-cost APAC manufacturers as well as Alli-
son Transmission. 
Marine & Industry Segment:   
The M&I segment presents a broader competitive environment, with firms from re-
gions such as China (if we exclude the military segment) exerting additional pres-
sure by offering lower prices while maintaining high product quality (especially in 
the Industry segment). This intensifies price competition and could lead to further 
margin compression for RENK. Many of RENK’s customers in this segment face sig-
nificant competitive pressures themselves, often requiring cost reductions from 
suppliers.  
Consequently, this drives a trend toward outsourcing production to low-cost coun-
tries, which RENK is constrained from following, again due to security-related re-
strictions. Nevertheless, as the EU navies significantly increase demand, e.g. seen 
at TKMS´s order backlog of 9x revenue, RENK will still profit due to these security-
related restrictions with only few competitors left in the Marine segment.

Slide Bearings segment 
The slide bearings market is highly competitive, with numerous established global 
players. RENK’s differentiation in this commoditized space depends largely on prod-
uct quality and long-standing customer relationships. However, persistent pricing 
pressure and intense competition continue to weigh on margins, and feedback from 
peers suggests limited growth potential in the SB segment over the medium term. 
Prominent competitors in the global bearing market include: NSK Ltd., Schaeffler 
AG, JTEKT Corporation, SKF, The Timken Company, Minebea Mitsumi Inc., NTN Cor-
poration, Dahler and MTU.

Suppliers 
Specific details about the concentration of RENK's supplier base or the ease of 
switching suppliers are not publicly disclosed. As RENK operated in a security sen-
sitive sector in it´s operating business the suppliers cannot come from countries 
like China, Russia etc…(only <EUR 1m are directly sourced from China (mwb. est.). 
Therefore, Renk needs to rely on rather expensive suppliers from the western re-
gions. 
Even though many competitors faced significant supply chain disruptions, RENK ap-
pears well-positioned to meet the growing demand for military equipment, with a 
supply chain that has remained resilient. This operational strength is reflected in 
RENK’s COGS, which averages ~ 77% of revenue with only small fluctuations. While 
this percentage shows only slight variation over time, its consistently high level un-
derscores the critical importance of effective cost management in a business model 
that is highly sensitive to rising input prices. Maintaining stable COGS amidst fluc-
tuating market conditions highlights RENK's robust procurement strategy and care-
ful supplier management.  
Due to economies of scale, as well as the increase of suppliers, we expect a slight 
decrease in COGS by -4.2 ppt until the final planning period.

Growth 
As a mission-critical supplier to OEMs such as Rheinmetall (RHM:GR), BAE Systems 
(BA:UK), and KNDS, RENK’s topline growth closely mirrors defense procurement 
trends and platform delivery cycles. With components integrated into systems 
across NATO members, RENK is well-positioned to benefit from the ongoing de-
fense super cycle, driven by decades of underinvestment and multi-year replenish-
ment needs. We expect a sales CAGR of 14.1% until 2032, including a drop in growth 
reflecting normalized long-term procurement programs. Moreover, the company 
generates ~30% of its revenues from high-margin maintenance and lifecycle sup-
port, providing long-term visibility even after vehicle delivery peaks.  
To support margin expansion, RENK is automating production, including robotic 
welding and material handling systems in its Augsburg plant. These initiatives are 
expected to enhance efficiency, particularly within the Vehicle Mobility segment, and 
support our assumption of a -4.2% decline in the COGS ratio. 
On the civilian side, RENK operates across a diversified portfolio, including LNG car-
riers, ferries, wind turbines, and heavy industry, with expected market growth of 
~3.5% CAGR through 2030 (mwb est.). While growth is significantly slower than in 
defense, this segment adds stability and limits sector concentration risk. 
Defense super cycle: 
Following the Cold War and the U.S. troop withdrawal from Iraq in 2011, global de-
fense budgets, particularly in Western countries, saw a decline. However, since the 
annexation of Crimea in 2014 by Russia and amid recent major conflicts, this trend 
has reversed with the majority of growth yet to come. Significant military deficits 
now need to be rebuilt, a process demanding substantial time and financial invest-
ment. 
In the past years, NATO countries saw increased military spending across nearly all 
members. The U.S. accounted for 68% of NATO’s total military expenditure in 2023, 
with a CAGR of 3.1% from 2014 to 2023. This was lower than NATO’s overall growth 
rate of 3.3% and Germany’s 4.4% growth, despite Germany only contributes 5.3% of 
NATO’s total budget. However, these growth rates are not comparable to the growth 
rates in the upcoming years. 
 
 
 
 
Many European countries now exceed the 2% GDP defense spending target amid 
ongoing war in Ukraine and rising geopolitical tensions. Poland leads with 4%, while 
others, including Germany, are rapidly increasing budgets. Modernization efforts 
span all domains to strengthen defense in the Baltic and North Sea regions. Fears 
of a potential U.S. withdrawal from NATO have further accelerated spending (even 
though not assumed in our model yet). The NATO has responded with a new goal 
of 3.5% of GDP by 2035, signaling a long-term shift toward greater military readi-
ness.

Impact of a potential end to the Ukrainian war 
A ceasefire in Ukraine would not reduce defense demand but rather shift it toward 
industrial build-up in the region. Ukraine would require a large-scale reconstruction 
of its army and navy, creating demand for modern land systems and naval plat-
forms. This process would likely be supported by NATO and EU partners, ensuring 
sustained financing and long-term commitments.  
The K2 program in Poland already illustrates how production could be localized in 
Eastern Europe, with Ukraine as a potential future hub. Rheinmetall has publicly ex-
pressed interest in building facilities in the region, highlighting the strategic im-
portance for Western defense suppliers.  
Overall, the modernization of Ukraine’s forces and broader rearmament in Eastern 
Europe will take decades, offering a structural growth driver for the industry. RENK 
will likely build an assembly line in Poland or Ukraine once the war ends as their 
systems are in nearly all the tanks used in the region which will increase revenues. 
 
 
Impact of drones on future demand 
The rise of drones has sparked debate about the future relevance of heavy armored 
platforms, yet we see continued demand for tanks and tracked vehicles.  
1) The costs of abandoning core competencies in armored propulsion are too high 
for NATO countries, making tanks an indispensable capability. 2) The newest gen-
erations of vehicles are increasingly equipped with active protection systems, elec-
tronic countermeasures, and armor upgrades to defend against drones. 3) Drones 
cannot replace tanks in regaining territory, as ground forces remain essential for 
offensive and defensive operations. The current conflict in Israel underlines how 
heavily such vehicles are still used in modern warfare, while the war in Ukraine is not 
directly comparable to a large-scale conflict in Central Europe.  
Overall, we consider the bigger structural risk not to come from drones, but from the 
possibility that future combat vehicles shift from tracked propulsion to wheeled so-
lutions, where propulsion costs are significantly lower (> -90%) and competition is 
broader.

New growth market – Electric engines 
Current propulsion systems primarily rely on fossil fuels. However, hybrid systems 
offer significant advantages in both the maritime and defense sectors, driving 
strong market growth. We expect the maritime hybrid propulsion market to grow at 
a CAGR of 17% through 2030, with the primary demand coming from engines in the 
75 kW – 7560 kW range, precisely the segment in which RENK operates.  
Similarly, the defense industry is transitioning toward hybrid propulsion systems, as 
seen in the latest generation of military vehicles. For example, the XM30 tank from 
Rheinmetall incorporates a hybrid propulsion system, though it currently utilizes Al-
lison’s eGen Force electric hybrid solution. This shift underscores the growing im-
portance of hybrid technology in military applications, a trend RENK is well posi-
tioned to capitalize on. 
As European nations continue to modernize their fleets, upcoming defense procure-
ment programs are increasingly likely to include hybrid propulsion systems. RENK 
is well-equipped to participate in these contracts, further strengthening its position 
in this emerging market. 
 
 
Slide bearings growth outlook  
RENK’s Slide Bearings segment, which contributed approximately EUR 170m in rev-
enue in 2024, is expected to deliver stable revenues over the coming years. The 
business serves a structurally mature customer base across sectors such as power 
generation, marine propulsion, rail transport, heavy industry, and energy infrastruc-
ture.  
Modest growth in this segment is supported by grid modernization efforts, turbine 
retrofits, and selective investment in LNG and marine infrastructure. However, the 
overall market remains saturated in key regions such as Europe, and growth is struc-
turally capped by low volume turnover, long asset cycles, and rising global competi-
tion, particularly from price-aggressive suppliers in Asia. As a result, we expect rev-
enue in this segment to grow at a low single-digit pace between 2025 and 2029, 
gradually flattening thereafter.  
While the Slide Bearings segment is unlikely to be a material growth driver for RENK 
at group level, it remains a strategically valuable business line with attractive char-
acteristics: low volatility, high engineering depth, and a defensible market position 
in critical infrastructure applications. We therefore see this segment contributing a 
consistent, if modest, layer of earnings support over the forecast period. 
Furthermore, management has confirmed that a sale is possible. We estimate such 
a transaction could contribute around EUR 350m to RENK.

Guidance & conclusion 
RENK has guided for a strong growth trajectory in the coming years, supported by 
robust defense procurement trends and a record-high order backlog. For FY 2025, 
management targets revenue of more than EUR 1.3bn, up ~15% yoy from the 2024 
base of EUR 1.1bn. Adjusted EBIT is expected in the range of EUR 210–235m, im-
plying an EBIT margin of 16–18%. Given the strong order execution so far, we expect 
results toward the upper end of this range in FY 2025. 
Looking further ahead, RENK has reaffirmed its medium-term ambition to reach EUR 
2.0bn in annual revenue by 2028, underpinned by an order book of EUR 5.5bn at year-
end 2024. Consensus already estimates 2.3bn in 2028. However, this is highly de-
pendent on KNDS’s ability to scale production, something KNDS has repeatedly 
struggled with, missing past opportunities to expand capacity. While the market ap-
pears to more than priced in KNDS’s ramp-up, we see this as very optimistic. 
In our view, RENK’s official guidance represents a credible base case that is con-
sistent with our analysis. We forecast revenues of EUR 3.0 bn by 2030, with EBIT 
margins improving to 20% from 2028 onwards. Our projections assume continued 
execution on key vehicle programs (Panther, Lynx), the ramp-up of major naval con-
tracts (F126, EPC, F127), and a steady increase in aftermarket share, reaching ap-
proximately 35% of sales in the terminal period. We currently do not factor in trans-
formational M&A or meaningful exposure to U.S. platforms, which remain sources 
of potential upside. Further upside could also arise if the U.S. reduces its European 
footprint amid heightened tensions in the Pacific. 
Order intake should remain strong. We expect major contracts in H1 2026 for Leop-
ard 2, Puma, PzH 2000, and Boxer, alongside new programs such as the IMBT (Italy’s 
Panther, with Rheinmetall and Leonardo), the AICS, and the K2 family. CAPEX is 
modeled at a steady <3%, as RENK’s production equipment inherited from 
Volkswagen remains modern, and the company currently operates only a one-shift 
system in final assembly (3 transmissions/day), leaving headroom for significant 
capacity expansion. 
We apply a long-term tax rate of 32%. Cost of sales is expected to improve gradually 
by 4.2ppt to 67%, driven by supply chain diversification (critical as RENK currently 
relies heavily on single-source suppliers in several defense systems). Investors 
should closely monitor new platforms in the EU due to the risk that more military 
vehicles could move away from tracked propulsion toward wheeled solutions (e.g. 
GTX Boxer), where propulsion costs are only ~1/15 of tracked systems and more 
competitors are present.

price – in this case the enterprise value of the company. Here, the adjusted FCF yield

Peer group analysis 
A peer group or comparable company (“comps”) analysis is a methodology that cal-
culates a company's relative value — how much it should be worth based on how it 
compares to other similar companies. Given that RENK Group AG differs quite sig-
nificantly in terms of size, focus, financial health and growth trajectory, we regard 
our peer group analysis merely as a support for other valuation methods. The peer 
group of RENK Group AG consists of the stocks displayed below. As of 7 October 
2025 the median market cap of the peer group was EUR 13,398m, compared to EUR 
8,853m for RENK Group AG. In the period under review, the peer group was less 
profitable than RENK Group AG. The expectations for sales growth are lower for the 
peer group than for RENK Group AG.

4. Information Concerning the Methods of Valuation/Update The determination 
of the fair value per share, i.e. the price target, and the resultant rating is done on 
the basis of the adjusted free cash flow (adj. FCF) method and on the basis of 
the discounted cash flow – DCF model. Furthermore, a peer group comparison 
is made. The adj. FCF method is based on the assumption that investors pur-
chase assets only at a price (enterprise value) at which the operating cash flow 
return after taxes on this investment exceeds their opportunity costs in the form 
of a hurdle rate. The operating cash flow is calculated as EBITDA less mainte-
nance capex and taxes. Within the framework of the DCF approach, the future 
free cash flows are calculated initially on the basis of a fictitious capital structure 
of 100% equity, i.e. interest and repayments on debt capital are not factored in 
initially. The adjustment towards the actual capital structure is done by discount-
ing the calculated free cash flows with the weighted average cost of capital 
(WACC), which takes into account both the cost of equity capital and the cost of 
debt. After discounting, the calculated total enterprise value is reduced by the 
interest-bearing debt capital in order to arrive at the equity value. Detailed infor-
mation on the valuation principles and methods used and the underlying assump-
tions can be found at https://www.mwb.-research.com.
